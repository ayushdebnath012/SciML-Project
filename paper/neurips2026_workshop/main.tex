\begin{abstract}
Causal time weighting and GradNorm loss balancing are standard equipment in
modern physics-informed neural network (PINN) training. On the 1D elastic wave
equation in heterogeneous media we find that, run exactly as specified,
\textbf{both hurt}: switching them off beats the full recipe in every one of
the $\AblPairCount$ architecture--material cells we measured, by
$\AblMinGain$--$\AblMaxGain\times$ (median $\AblMedianGain\times$), and takes
the best network from $\PinnRecipeBestErr\%$ to $\AblBestErr\%$. Under the
recipe, $\PinnRecipeAboveHundred$ of $\PinnRecipeCount$ runs score at or above
the $100\%$ that predicting zero earns; without it, none do. The mechanism is
measurable rather than mysterious: in $\FrontierStalled$ of $\FrontierTotal$
causal runs the schedule's frontier stalls, leaving part of the time domain
weighted at exactly zero for the whole of training, and GradNorm independently
settles at a weighting that puts the PDE residual three orders of magnitude
below the boundary term. Only $\FrontierComplete$ of those runs open all
sixteen slabs: causal weighting is conditional on the optimiser reaching the
early slabs, and when that fails it is much worse than not weighting at all. We
reach this by way of a control the PINN literature rarely runs: a characterised
classical
baseline on the same problem, same grid, same metric. Building one turns up a
second result --- second-order finite differences and linear finite elements
both stall at $\FDFloor\%$ on all three materials, a floor set by the
first-order absorbing boundary rather than the interior stencil, which any PINN
scored against such a reference silently inherits. Even after the ablation, the
best network's error is $\AblVsFDRatio\times$ that of a finite-difference solve
costing \SI{\FDRefSecs}{\second} on one CPU core, so the headline is not that
PINNs have been fixed. It is that a widely used training recipe is being
credited for accuracy it is in fact removing, and that the diagnostic which
reveals this ($w_{\min}$, already computed by every causal implementation)
costs nothing to report.
\end{abstract}
